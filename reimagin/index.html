01 · Motivation

Multimodal models still reason in words

Chain-of-thought taught language models to think in intermediate steps. Multimodal models inherited the inputs, so they can look at images, but not the manipulation: their reasoning stays textual, describing a picture in words rather than changing it.

Prior work adds specialist vision tools such as depth estimation, segmentation, and object detection, so a model can act on pixels. But each tool performs one fixed operation, and many transformations a task needs have no tool at all.

02 · The idea

Make the tool a generator

Instruction-following image generators change what a visual tool can be. They perform any operation that can be expressed in natural language, so the set of possible operations is open-ended.

ReImaGin gives the model a single, general tool. At any step it can call generate_image with a prompt and reason over the result, repeating until it can answer.

Reason→ generate_image→ Observe→ Answer

06 · Test-time scaling

Scale the image, not the text

Generating images opens a new axis of test-time scaling. Rather than only thinking for longer in words, ReImaGin can imagine the same scene several times and keep the best picture.

For one input and prompt, it samples N = 10 candidate generations, then an MLLM selector compares them and keeps the most faithful one to reason over.

The payoff is largest on multi-view spatial reasoning, where stitching two views and synthesizing a top-down map is geometrically hard and single generations vary a lot. Selecting the best of ten lifts spatial accuracy on every model.

Spatial reasoning accuracy (MMSI, %). Test-time scaling is applied only to this task, where generated layouts vary most across samples.

For hard geometric tasks, improving the quality of the imagined visual state matters more than adding reasoning steps on a weak one.

07 · Strategy discovery

Can the strategy be discovered automatically?

Choosing which visualization to generate, and when, is itself a strategy. Until now it was written by hand for each task. So we ask whether it can be found automatically: propose candidate strategies, evaluate them on a small dev set, keep the best, and refine.

1 · Propose→ 2 · Evaluate→ 3 · Keep the best→ 4 · Refine↻

REPEATED EACH ROUND

The automatically discovered strategies recover most of the handcrafted gains with no human-written examples, and often rediscover the handcrafted policy on their own.

Gemini-3.1-Pro accuracy (%). Automatically discovered strategies close most of the gap between no strategy and the handcrafted policy.

They transfer across models. Prompts discovered with Gemini-3.1-Pro, applied unchanged to Qwen-3.5-27B, still beat text-only reasoning and the specialist baseline on most tasks (e.g. tracing 35.7 → 55.9, collision 51.8 → 54.4).
